Hey! This is Nikky. I am a researcher with a background in computer science, philosophy, and cognitive science. My work is motivated by concepts from philosophy and cognitive science to understand AI systems and human cognition.

Currently, I am pursuing a MS at Brown, working on interpretability inspired by human perception and ways of interaction. Previously, I was a researcher at the Human and Machine Learning Lab at NYU, advised by Dr. Brenden Lake. I was also a trainee in the NIH funded Computational Neuroscience Training Program at NYU. I wrote my thesis in philosophy of mind and language, exploring whether language models can establish intrinsic meaning in their representations.

Outside of research, I have been continuously making art. I am also an active mountaineer and traveler. So far, I've solo-backpacked to more than 20 destinations. I have summited 5,000m and 4,000m peaks and will be summiting Mount Blanc with friends in the summer of 2026.

Previously I was an active cyclist who solo bikepacked across New York State from Manhattan to Niagara Falls in 7 days in 2024.

Research

Ensemble Perception in Vision Transformers (2026)

Investigating whether vision transformers encode scene-level summary statistics across spatial tokens, drawing on psychophysical theories of ensemble perception in human vision.

PI: Dr. Thomas Serre

Stylistic Accommodation in Language Models (2026)

Examining whether alignment training introduces representational priors that affect models' ability to accommodate to interlocutor style in conversation.

Optimizing Environments Through Goal Interpretation (2025)

Studied how varying levels of abstraction in goal description impact human understanding and creativity through behavioral experiments and computational models.

PI: Dr. Brenden Lake

Thesis: The Vector Grounding Problem (2025)

Whether language models can establish intrinsic meaning in their representations beyond mere ascriptions by external interpreters.
